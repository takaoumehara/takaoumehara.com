Decide what you're making, before you argue about the tech.

interactive-experience-skills is three Claude Code skills for products that read human movement with a camera or a sensor. A director skill settles the one question that decides everything — is this something people experience, or a tool that makes people better? — and only then hands the work to the right specialist. The most common failure in this field is arguing about TouchDesigner versus Unreal before that question has an answer.

GitHub ↗

Answers in lumens and milliseconds, not in "immersive".

5,000–8,000 lumens to project three metres wide in a dark room. Feedback on a body movement has to return within 100 ms or it stops feeling like your own movement. A front-facing camera cannot measure how deep a step is, so you need a side view. About 96,000 characters of this, loaded only when the current question needs it. The skills don't write code — what comes back is a design decision: what to build, roughly what equipment and how much of it, what to validate first, and what not to build yet. They are written in Japanese; Claude reads and applies them in any language, so you can work entirely in English.
